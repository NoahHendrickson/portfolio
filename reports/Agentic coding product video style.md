# Stage the real app, move the camera once

The Claude developer videos work because they barely animate anything. The UI is real, or a faithful scripted render of it, and the app's own behavior does the moving: a caret types, an answer streams, rows re-sort, a question card collapses to its answer. Over that sits one or two decisive camera moves and a cursor that only shows up when something needs clicking. The liked Projects clip is **18.6 s, 1920×1080 at 60 fps, one continuous shot with no hard cuts, on a flat `#181818` field, effectively silent, with no title or end card** ([video A](https://video.twimg.com/amplify_video/2121373287884419073/vid/avc1/1920x1080/rUkftd_yGittV8xV.mp4)). Across the wider field (Cursor, Codex, Linear, Raycast, Warp), the films people praise share restraint and one memorable idea, such as Raycast's unbroken pan or Linear's sound on Send. They do not share effects, and the Screen Studio template (dark gradient, floating window, auto-zoom on every click) now reads as generic. For no3y Code, this means the user's existing pipeline is already the right kind of tool: a seeded, offline copy of the real app, frame-stepped over Chrome DevTools at 3 to 4x on a virtual clock. The gains are in choreography and editing, not in a new motion tool: one idea per clip, scripted app state that tells the story, at most one eased camera move, time compressed by scripting rather than by speeding up playback, and bookends built from no3y's own marks (the leaf mark, the Petal bots, Geist) rather than Anthropic's serif, clay mascot and spark. One gap is worth knowing up front. Nothing public says what tools Anthropic uses, the audio could only be measured for loudness, not heard, and the easing values were read off frames rather than taken from a source.

## Anthropic's clips are one staged shot of the real product

Four recent @ClaudeDevs videos were pulled and frame-analyzed (all 1920×1080, 16:9, H.264). They fall into **two clear tiers**. Feature drops run 15 to 20 s with no bookends and near-silent audio. A named launch runs about 46 s and adds a title card, an end sting and music.

The "animation" in the liked clip is mostly the product behaving. The clip opens about 2× into the chat column, mid-conversation, with three sub-thread rows already pulsing. "where are we on everything?" types in at **roughly 4 to 6 characters per half second** with a blinking caret. An orange "Infusing 2s" indicator counts up, then the answer streams line by line, each line fading up with its trailing clause resolving from grey. At about 8.4 s comes **the only camera move: a pull-back from roughly 2× to 1× in about 0.5 s, with most of the travel in the first 0.2 to 0.3 s**. That reads as a strong expo or quart ease-out with no overshoot. The camera is locked before and after. Then a status update arrives, Threads rows re-sort between "Working" and "Ready for review", and a question card ("Where should the rate limiter keep its counts?") gets answered by a macOS arrow cursor that has been absent for the first half of the clip. The card collapses to the chosen answer with a check, and the clip ends on the app with the cursor at rest ([video A](https://video.twimg.com/amplify_video/2121373287884419073/vid/avc1/1920x1080/rUkftd_yGittV8xV.mp4)).

A few traits recur across all four. **Nothing floats.** The app fills the frame edge to edge with no wallpaper, device frame, shadow or gradient, and the shots alternate between a tight crop (about 2× on a column, about 3× on a menu) and the full window. Video B isolates a component by blurring and fading everything else while the menu scales up onto the empty dark field, a push-in that subtracts context instead of just magnifying ([video B](https://video.twimg.com/amplify_video/2124963618194337792/vid/avc1/1920x1080/2tcgCzOZbSAJKd8S.mp4)). There are no Ken Burns drifts, no 3D tilts, no callout arrows and no captions. For emphasis, video C uses a text-selection highlight over the key clause instead of a callout, and a small grey "Illustrative example" caption admits the scenario is staged ([video C](https://video.twimg.com/amplify_video/2124957499291832320/vid/avc1/1920x1080/HG-UQUFghkiAt4x1.mp4)). Every clip shares one fictional, mundane world (a storefront app, "Acme Corp", PR #412, a teammate "jordan"), so the posts read as one universe. The post text carries the explanation, which lets the video stay wordless.

The brand appears only at the edges. Inside the clips, color is functional (orange thinking dots, green checks, amber "waiting"). The warmth comes from the bookends of the launch tier: a centered serif headline set small (cap height about 3% of frame), a pixel-art clay mascot with pixel props, and a closing sting that draws the spark and reveals "Claude Code" letter by letter. Anthropic's identity pairs Styrene with Tiempos ([Geist](https://geist.co/work/anthropic)), and the card serif looks consistent with that, though this wasn't confirmed. **No source credits these videos or names their tools.** Anthropic has an in-house "Motion Designer, Core Brand" role ([Built In](https://builtin.com/job/motion-designer-core-brand/9385326)), and the consistency across posts made days apart suggests an in-house pipeline. Pin-sharp text at a 2× crop, perfectly even typing and curated fake data all point to a scripted render of the real UI rather than an ad-hoc screen recording. That reading is an inference, not a fact.

## The praised benchmarks each commit to a single idea

Outside Anthropic, there is no single house style, but three registers recur. **Founder or engineer on camera** is the first: Cursor 3 (90 s, co-founders on camera, product at 16 s), Cursor for iOS (74 s), Warp 2.0's CEO-led ADE launch, and Google Antigravity, which put the ex-Windsurf team on camera with a levitating couch on set and was self-reported at 6M+ X views in 72 hours ([Demosmith](https://demosmith.ai/blog/saas-product-launch-video-examples), [Warp](https://www.warp.dev/blog/reimagining-coding-agentic-development-environment), [madebytiger](https://www.madebytiger.com/portfolio/google-antigravity)). **Wordless UI films with music or foley** are the second: Cursor 2.0 (64 s, 60 fps, music only, the editor staying on screen "as one continuous task" broken by two captions), Linear Agent (55 s, 60 fps), Raycast's 2026 teaser (39 s), and Replit's Skale-made films ([AgentsORG corpus](https://github.com/AgentsORG/design-engineering/blob/main/docs/research/launch-films/README.md)). **Story films where the product arrives late** are the third: Raycast for Windows reveals the product at 46 s against a median of 8 s ([Demosmith](https://demosmith.ai/blog/saas-product-launch-video-examples)). OpenAI's Codex is the outlier. It launches by livestream and blog, backed by how-to libraries and, per the corpus, short silent 10 s UI loops on social ([OpenAI developer videos](https://developers.openai.com/videos), [AgentsORG corpus](https://github.com/AgentsORG/design-engineering/blob/main/docs/research/launch-films/README.md)).

What the cited praise rewards is specific. **Linear's Send sound sits about +14.5 dB over the sub-bass, with a musical key change on Send.** In other words, the key action is marked by sound, not by visuals ([AgentsORG corpus](https://github.com/AgentsORG/design-engineering/blob/main/docs/research/launch-films/README.md)). **Raycast's camera trucks continuously, accelerating from 3 to 8% of frame width per second**, a pan that "never breaks frame" and leads the eye from search bar to result ([AgentsORG corpus](https://github.com/AgentsORG/design-engineering/blob/main/docs/research/launch-films/README.md), [advids](https://advids.co/blog/saas-feature-launch)). Linear Releases "works because the shape is the feature," with release branches drawn as light ([Demosmith](https://demosmith.ai/blog/saas-product-launch-video-examples)). Across 18 curated acclaimed launch films, **16 open product-first and 14 pull sound out before the reveal so it lands on the drop** ([AgentsORG corpus](https://github.com/AgentsORG/design-engineering/blob/main/docs/research/launch-films/README.md)). Frame rate tracks the register: UI-true films run at 60 fps, filmic and live-action ones at 24.

The cliché is just as clear. The Screen Studio look (a dark gradient, a rounded window with a soft shadow, auto-zoom on every click) has become shorthand for "polished SaaS demo", and free one-click tools now reproduce it ([wildandfreetools](https://wildandfreetools.com/blog/screen-studio-alternative-free-polished-demos/)). Agent skills now prompt Remotion or HyperFrames launch videos into existence ([launch-video-craft](https://github.com/jammaru/launch-video-craft)), and Kiln found that without a style guide "you get very generic motion graphics" ([Kiln](https://kiln.tech/blog/we_made_our_launch_video_in_claude_code)). The real problem for agent products is that **agents are slow and their output is text**. The films that work compress time: Cursor 2.0 holds one continuous task, and Claude's clips cut to a finished state rather than showing the wait. A backdrop with character also stands out against the template. Cursor's site sets its UI on painted landscape wallpaper, not gradients ([cursor.com](https://cursor.com)). That is the same instinct as no3y's Jolly Petal piece, and a reason to keep it.

## The craft numbers converge on restraint

The sourced craft guidance lines up with what the Claude clips do. On **camera**, the guidance is one primary move per shot plus at most one supporting move, and zoom of 1.3× to 2× (2.5× is the ceiling for a single small element), since past about 2× small moves turn into lurches ([Screenify](https://www.screenify.studio/blog/2026-04-10-auto-zoom-screen-recording), [ZoomCap](https://zoomcap.live/blog/why-screen-recordings-look-bad), [AppLaunchFlow](https://www.applaunchflow.com/blog/animated-device-mockups)). Start the move about 0.5 s before the action and keep each zoom under about 5 s ([Recorded](https://recorded.app/en/blog/creating-effective-product-demos/)). Use **"three zoom moments per minute, tops"**, and none during navigation ([Tenzen](https://tenzen.studio/blog/smooth-zoom-pan-screen-recording/)). All of this comes from screen-recorder vendors, so treat it as working heuristics. The underlying principle is the useful part: move the camera between actions and hold it still while the UI acts. That is exactly the liked clip's structure. The user's own recorded taste is stricter still. No3y Code videos have been one padded wide shot with no zoom since Nov 4 2026. Four zooms in a 36 s video read as "too much zooming in and out", and the guidance is one or two per video at most: up to 1.6× on a small focus, about 1.25× on a group, eased in and out with no overshoot ([product-video skill](/Users/noey/.claude/skills/product-video/SKILL.md)).

For **motion inside the frame**, UI should move like shipped UI. Use strong ease-outs such as `cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)` for enters and exits and `cubic-bezier(0.77, 0, 0.175, 1)` for on-screen travel, never ease-in. Keep durations at 150 to 300 ms for small UI and up to about 500 ms for panels. Enters start from scale 0.9 to 0.97, never 0, with 30 to 80 ms stagger and spring bounce at 0.3 or below ([Kowalski STANDARDS.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/emilkowalski/skills/main/skills/review-animations/STANDARDS.md)). Since no3y Code is the real app, it already has its own values, and the video should leave them alone. For **typing and streaming**, about 60 ms per character (roughly 16 cps) reads as human typing ([TextToolbox](https://texttoolbox.net/typewriter-effect-generator/)), and a production chat UI streams at **5 ms per character (about 200 cps) and stays readable** ([Upstash](https://upstash.com/blog/smooth-streaming)). The user's take kit already types at an uneven ~15 cps and draws its caret on the virtual clock, because a real-clock caret flickers and makes the whole video read as sped up ([product-video skill](/Users/noey/.claude/skills/product-video/SKILL.md)). **Cursor** paths should be short gentle arcs that move fast and nearly stop before the click, with a small ripple at the contact point ([Demotion](https://trydemotion.com/blog/cursor-click-animation-after-effects)).

**Text and sound** rules follow from muted autoplay. X videos autoplay muted ([Meltwater](https://www.meltwater.com/en/blog/guide-to-posting-videos-to-twitter)), so Anthropic's choice to carry the meaning in the post text and keep feature clips wordless is coherent rather than lazy. Any text card needs reading time, about 15 to 20 cps plus half a second to land ([Netflix reading speed](https://backlothelp.netflix.com/hc/en-us/articles/115001352212-How-is-reading-speed-measured-Do-punctuation-and-spaces-count)), which puts a 40-character headline at 2.5 to 4 s. When sound is used, use a small palette of 3 to 5 SFX under 1 s each, each tied to a cause and effect ([Avocado AI](https://www.avocadoai.co/blog/how-to/ai-sound-effects-product-demos-2026)), with the completion chime on the visible ready state rather than the click ([OpenDesign](https://open-design.ai/blog/sound-design-product-videos/)). On **length**, hero films have contracted to 40 to 70 s, with social hooks at 6 to 15 s ([Composition](https://www.composition.agency/insights/saas-launch-video-trends-2026)). Anthropic's 15 to 20 s feature clips sit between the two. On **aspect ratio**, every product video sampled from @ClaudeDevs is 16:9, and no vertical or square cuts were seen. X recommends 16:9 or 1:1 ([X developer docs](https://docs.x.com/x-api/media/quickstart/best-practices)).

## Captured real UI beats recreation for a solo designer-engineer

Production tools fall into three families. **Keyframed motion apps** rebuild UI from design files: After Effects with Overlord (about $31 to 38 a month plus $75), Cavalry (free for individuals since April 2026, [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/motion-design-software-cavalry-is-now-free)), Jitter, Figma Motion and Rive. They are fastest to art-direct by eye and best at kinetic type and stylized UI, but they drift from the product as it changes. Figma's Brand Studio built its whole 3D-transforms launch film on the canvas ([Figma blog](https://www.figma.com/blog/how-3d-transforms-and-figmas-agent-brought-a-launch-video-to-life/)). Rive exports MP4, but its strength is interactive in-product animation ([Rive docs](https://rive.app/docs/editor/exporting/exporting-for-video-and-static-design)). **Code-driven renderers** draw real or replica components frame by frame: Remotion (free up to 3 people, [pricing](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/pricing)), HeyGen's HyperFrames, which "seeks each frame in headless Chrome and encodes the result with FFmpeg" ([GitHub](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes)), and Motion Canvas (MIT). **Auto-polish recorders** such as Screen Studio ($9 a month yearly, [pricing](https://screen.studio/pricing)) add zoom, cursor smoothing and a backdrop to real captures, but they cannot restage UI states or retime interactions. The pattern professionals converge on is a hybrid. **Interface, type and numbers come from code. Organic and illustrative layers are hand-animated. The two are joined through transparent ProRes 4444** ([Dardo Studio](https://dardo.studio/en/blog/remotion-video/)).

The user's pipeline is the third option the notes call out: frame-stepped capture of the real app. It sits between the families and avoids the weakness of each. It runs the actual Electron app on a seeded, offline profile, drives it over CDP with a virtual clock that steps `Date`, UI timers and Web Animations by 1/60 s per frame, captures at 3×, and composites the window onto a wallpaper with its own shadows and rims ([product-video skill](/Users/noey/.claude/skills/product-video/SKILL.md), [project CLAUDE.md](/Users/noey/Developer/portfolio/CLAUDE.md)). That gives true app behavior, including Flat Glass vibrancy laid over native sidebar captures, re-timeable zooms (marked during recording, applied in `render.mjs`), and pixel-sharp text at any crop. **That last property is what makes Anthropic's 2× crops look clean, and the pipeline already has it.** No keyframe tool will give Petals' real digest, the real queued-run row or the real bead ring for less effort. Rebuilding them in After Effects would be the copying-the-surface trap: it imitates a look instead of showing the product. Where a hand-animated layer earns its place is the bookends. The portfolio's `IntroSplash` already draws the four-leaf mark square by square and types "no3y" (`introTimeline.ts`, the `fan` preset with the `sweep` label), which makes it a ready-made logo sting in code.

Delivery has one trap. The web build serves a 10-bit HEVC Petals clip because 8-bit H.264 bands the dark glass's frosted gradients ([project CLAUDE.md](/Users/noey/Developer/portfolio/CLAUDE.md)). **X's documented ingest is H.264 High, YUV 4:2:0, ≤60 fps, at least 5,000 kbps video and AAC-LC audio** ([X developer docs](https://docs.x.com/x-api/media/quickstart/best-practices)), and third-party guides disagree on whether HEVC is accepted at all ([Konvrt](https://konvrt.dev/blog/x-twitter-image-video-specs-2026)). The 10-bit fix therefore won't survive the trip to X. The X master needs its anti-banding carried in the pixels: the ordered dither or light grain the stills already use, encoded at a generous bitrate. This is an inference from the specs and hasn't been tested on X's re-encode.

## Borrow the grammar, not the costume

The line between principle and imitation falls cleanly. Anthropic's principles are generic film grammar that predates them. Their costume is a specific combination of color, type, mascot and sting that viewers already recognize as Claude.

| Borrow (principle) | Would be copying Anthropic's look |
|---|---|
| One continuous take of the real UI; no hard cuts in a feature clip | A flat `#181818` field with the window full-bleed and no backdrop. No3y's wallpaper stage (Jolly Petal) is its own signature, so keep it |
| The app's own micro-motion carries the story (typing, streaming, re-sorts, cards collapsing) | Their "Infusing Ns" orange thinking dots or similar copy; use no3y's Rain / bead ring and its own status chips |
| Cursor appears only when something needs clicking | The exact cold-open beat of the liked clip (tight chat, "where are we on everything?", pull-back, rate-limiter question) |
| One decisive eased camera move, or a blur-out that isolates one component | Serif title cards, a pixel mascot gag that boards a vehicle, a spark-then-wordmark sting |
| Two tiers: wordless 15 to 25 s feature drops; a longer launch cut with bookends and sound | A fictional "Storefront / Acme / jordan" world; build no3y's own recurring world instead |
| Post copy explains, video shows | Text-selection highlight as the emphasis device: borderline, and fine only if it's something no3y itself does |
| A small honesty label when a scenario is staged | Their wording, "Illustrative example"; say it in your own voice |

The pixel mascot is the one element that needs care. Pixel art is native to no3y (the Petal bots, the rail's pixel faces, the dithered wallpaper, the leaf mark), so using it isn't copying. **Copying would be Anthropic's structure**: a centered serif card with a mascot standing above it that then acts out a gag. The no3y version keeps the bots *inside the product*, where they already live as sidebar cards and in the chat header, and lets the leaf mark's square-by-square build be the sting. It sets any card type in Geist, the portfolio and app face, not a serif.

## Conclusion

The style the user likes is less "animation" than it looks. Most of the motion is the product's own state machine, staged and timed. That moves the creative work out of a motion tool and into the **seed and the take script**: which threads exist, what streams in and how fast, which card asks the question, when the bot returns. The user's offline mock studio and virtual clock are, in effect, the motion tool. The camera's job is subtraction, not spectacle. The one move in the liked clip pulls back to *reveal* context instead of zooming in to show off. That fits the user's no-zoom taste better than the 2× crops do, and it suggests that when no3y's wide shot needs focus, blurring away the rest of the window beats magnifying it.

The second implication concerns agent speed. Every agentic product video has to hide the wait, and the honest way to do that is to script time (streaming at 100 to 200 cps, SIGSTOP pauses, a status that arrives "later") rather than speed-ramping playback, which the user already rejects. The third is that a backdrop with character has become the differentiator in a genre full of dark gradients and flat greys. Jolly Petal is the part of no3y's videos that Anthropic's can't share, so it should stay.

## Recipe: the next no3y Code feature video

This is a 20 to 24 s feature drop for X and the portfolio. The example story is a Petal bot returning with a digest whose ask is answered from the composer, but the beats fit any single feature (queued message steering, question card, model menu).

**Concept and world (before any capture)**

1. **Pick one idea.** Write it as a single sentence the post will carry, e.g. "Petals check in on their own and hand you the decision." If it needs "and also", it is two clips.
2. **Build a recurring fictional world** in the mock studio (one project name, a few PR numbers, one or two teammate names) and reuse it in every future clip. It must be your own invented world, not storefront / Acme / jordan.
3. **Write prompts in the user's voice**: short and casual. Ask the user for the exact wording rather than drafting it.
4. **Start from a seeded scene** (`node mock.mjs up <scene>`), with stale schedules reseeded and the app stopped first, so nothing fires against real models.

**Beats and timings (one continuous take, 60 fps master)**

| Time | Beat | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 to 0.5 s | Cold open on a state that already reads as no3y: sidebar with Petals, a working thread's bead ring or Rain | Frame 0 is the poster; it must say "agentic app" with no motion |
| 0.5 to 3.0 s | Type a short prompt (under 40 characters) at an uneven ~15 cps, virtual-clock caret | Cursor hidden until needed; text field already focused |
| 3.0 to 8.0 s | The agent or bot works; status chip changes; reply or digest streams at about 100 to 200 cps | Script the stream rate in the seed; SIGSTOP the backend through any wait; no playback speed-up |
| 8.0 to 8.6 s | **The one camera move, if any**: a pull-back from about 1.25 to 1.5× to the padded wide shot, cubic ease, no overshoot. Alternative: dim or blur the rest of the window around one component | Starts about 0.5 s before the next action; camera locked before and after |
| 8.6 to 17 s | The payoff: the cursor glides in on a short arc (about 0.4 s), clicks the ask or option, the card collapses, a row moves between status groups | Faint press ripple; one action at a time; nothing else animating |
| 17 to 21 s | Hold on the settled state (2 to 3 s), cursor resting on a header, not on a sidebar card (cards open a preview after 150 ms) | Hold with sub-pixel cursor drift so frames keep coming |
| 21 to 24 s | Optional, portfolio cut only: cross-dissolve to the `IntroSplash` leaf-mark build and "no3y" | Leave it off the X feature drop |

**Camera rules**

1. One camera move per clip at most, zero by default.
2. Up to 1.6× for one small element and about 1.25× for a group of controls.
3. Move only between actions. Never move during a menu opening or a navigation.
4. Never transform the live page. Mark the move during recording and apply it in `render.mjs`.

**Keep from the pipeline**

1. Seeded offline app, CDP frame-stepping at 3 to 4× on the virtual clock, the drawn caret and the human-cursor model.
2. Flat Glass over native sidebar captures, the Jolly Petal wallpaper with nearest-neighbor scaling, the window shadows and rims.
3. A 4K master and a 1440p render, plus the 10-bit HEVC and H.264 pair for the web.

**Change or add**

1. **Add an X master**: 1920×1080, 16:9, H.264 High 4:2:0, 60 fps, about 10 to 15 Mbps (above X's 5 Mbps floor), AAC-LC 48 kHz, faststart. Bake a light dither or grain into the frames so the glass doesn't band after X's 8-bit re-encode, then check it with the high-pass test before posting.
2. **Keep the X cut wordless and nearly silent.** Put the explanation in the post text.
3. **Give the portfolio or launch cut sound**: 3 to 5 SFX under 1 s (send, card resolve, bot return), with the completion sound on the visible settled state. Pull the bed out just before the payoff and drop it back in on it.
4. **Add a small "seeded demo" style label** in Geist, in your own wording, whenever the scenario is staged.
5. **Use bookends for launches only.** For a 40 to 50 s named launch, use a Geist title card held 2.5 to 4 s, three or four one-idea beats like the one above joined by dissolves, Petal bots appearing only as they do in the app, and the leaf-mark build as the sting. No serif, no mascot gag, no spark-style wordmark reveal.
6. **Check before showing**: a contact sheet at a dozen timestamps plus one full-size crop, and a slowed (2 to 5×) review of the camera move to confirm it settles without overshoot.
