# Product and launch videos of non-Anthropic agentic coding / AI dev tools (and design-led benchmarks), ~mid-2025 to Nov 2026

Scope note for the report writer: this pass made about 15 search/fetch calls. Coverage is strong for Cursor, Linear, Raycast, Arc, Figma, Google Antigravity, Warp and Replit. It is thin or missing for Windsurf, Factory, Amp, Zed, Conductor, v0/Vercel Ship, GitHub Copilot, Google Jules and Figma Config. I did not watch any of the videos frame by frame myself. Everything below comes from fetched pages: company sites, an open-source research corpus (AgentsORG "launch-films"), agency/vendor blog breakdowns and one creative-lead portfolio. Treat the vendor blogs as their authors' own analysis, not neutral review.

## Per tool: format, length, framing, camera, type, cursor, pacing, sound, distribution

### Takeaway
No single house style exists. The tools split into three registers:
1. Founder or engineer on camera, cut with product: Cursor 3, Cursor for iOS, Warp 2.0, Google Antigravity.
2. Product-only UI motion films with music or foley and kinetic type: Cursor 2.0, Linear, Raycast, Figma, Replit's studio-made films.
3. Brand or story films where the product appears late: Raycast for Windows, Linear Releases, Arc.

Most of the companies in scope make their films in-house. Where an outside studio is named, it is a boutique: Skale for Replit and Cognition, Studio Freight for Perplexity, Ordinary Folk for Google Gemini, BUCK per a brief for Anthropic.

- "Cursor for iOS" (74 s): an engineer on a sofa shows three ways he uses the app, cutting between him talking and close-ups of the phone. ### Inferences
- Aspect ratio: every film above is published on YouTube or X as a landscape upload, and none of the sources mention vertical cuts. I infer 16:9 is the default, but I did not verify it.
- Frame rate tracks the register: UI-only films run 60 fps (Cursor 2.0, Linear Agent, Material 3), while live-action and story films run 24 fps (Arc, Notion, Devin Voice). A 24 fps UI film reads more "filmic", a 60 fps one more "product-true".
- Two kinds of tool lean on people rather than polish. Big-lab tools (Codex, Antigravity) lean on livestreams, people and humour. Startups after a credibility halo (Cursor 3, Warp 2.0) lean on founders. Design-led benchmarks (Linear, Raycast, Figma) lean on wordless, sound-designed UI films.
- Cursor shows its product on painted landscape wallpaper on the site, not on gradients. That is a deliberate break from the generic gradient-backdrop look, though I haven't verified how it treats backdrops in its videos.

### Gaps
- **Windsurf** (pre-Google), **Factory (Droids)**, **Sourcegraph Amp**, **Zed**, **Conductor**, **Vercel v0 / Vercel Ship**, **GitHub Copilot agent mode**, **Google Jules** and **Figma Config** keynote films: no sources found within budget. These need targeted searches of each company's X and YouTube accounts.
- Not verified for any tool: cursor treatment (enlarged, smoothed, hidden), exact typefaces in overlays, or whether a film is a real capture or a recreated UI. Linear Releases is the one confirmed "stylised" recreation.
- No per-film aspect-ratio data in any source.
- Engagement figures beyond Antigravity's self-reported 6M views, and the corpus's "looks promoted" flags.

## Which are widely praised, and why

### Takeaway
The praise that can be cited goes to Linear (wordless, sound-designed, typographic), Raycast (one continuous camera; narrative restraint), Arc (founder candour) and Antigravity (people and humour at Google scale). The reasons given are restraint, sound design used as an interface, and authenticity, not visual effects. Most "praise" sources are agency or vendor listicles, so independent design criticism is scarce.

### Cited Findings
- Linear's agent-era films "push minimal even further": UI footage, ambient sound only, no narration, sharp typography. — [advids](https://advids.co/blog/saas-feature-launch)
- In a timed analysis of nine launches, Raycast and Linear "break the usual pattern" on purpose, for audiences that already know them. Raycast for Windows reveals the product at 46 s against a median of 8 s. — [Demosmith](https://demosmith.ai/blog/saas-product-launch-video-examples)
- Linear Releases "works because the shape is the feature": release branches drawn as light. — [Demosmith](https://demosmith.ai/blog/saas-product-launch-video-examples)
- Arc: candour, with Miller "isn't afraid to show doubt or hesitation". — [Failory](https://newsletter.failory.com/p/lights-camera-arction)
- Antigravity: "scrappy and funny… kept it feeling real even at Google's scale". This is self-reported by the creative lead. — [madebytiger](https://www.madebytiger.com/portfolio/google-antigravity)
- The AgentsORG corpus curates 18 "acclaimed" launch films (Cursor 2.0, Linear Agent, Raycast, Arc, Figma ×2, Notion ×2, Granola, Framer, Perplexity, Google ×3, Spline, Apple, Anthropic ×2). It flags four as possibly paid-promoted, so "acclaimed" partly reflects reach. — [AgentsORG corpus](https://github.com/AgentsORG/design-engineering/blob/main/docs/research/launch-films/README.md)

### Inferences
- Videos that rely on visual polish alone don't show up in the sources as praised. The films people name have one memorable idea each: Linear's colour inversion on the last click, Releases' branches drawn as light, Raycast's unbroken pan, Arc's honesty.

### Gaps
- I found no tweets or threads quoting designers praising specific coding-tool videos (X wasn't searched directly). There is also no reception data for Cursor 2.0 beyond the replay and promotion notes.

## Cross-cutting patterns: premium vs generic, and clichés

### Takeaway
"Premium" in this set reads as:
- the real product shown within seconds (or, deliberately, very late)
- one continuous camera or one visual metaphor
- sound design synced to UI events (foley, a drop on the reveal, a key change on Send)
- type built word by word
- a short runtime, 30–70 s

"Generic" reads as the Screen Studio template: an auto-zoom on every click, a screen recording on a dark gradient with rounded corners and a soft shadow, a stock music bed, and a problem-montage-solution-CTA beat sheet.

### Cited Findings
- 16 of 18 acclaimed films open product-first. 14 of 18 subtract sound before the reveal and drop on it. Type is built word by word (27/29 Skale films), often with the newest word highlighted (15/29), and transitions zoom through. Acclaimed films cut about 14 times a minute against about 4 for Skale UI films. — [AgentsORG corpus](https://github.com/AgentsORG/design-engineering/blob/main/docs/research/launch-films/README.md)
- The median launch shows the real product within 8 s, and 8 of 9 do within 16 s. Lengths run 30–90 s. — [Demosmith](https://demosmith.ai/blog/saas-product-launch-video-examples)
- A vendor's "4-beat" spec: hook 0–4 s (UI cold open or kinetic type on a sound transient), problem 4–12 s, solution 12–34 s at 2–3× speed with orthographic 3D pans and spring motion, CTA 34–45 s. It prefers tactile foley (key switches, sub drops, whooshes) to stock music. Loudness -13.8 to -11.9 LUFS, 122–128 BPM. Caveat: the author sells the archive, criteria are undefined, and the figures are internally inconsistent. — [ShipScenes on DEV](https://dev.to/shipscenes/we-analyzed-720-ai-startup-launch-videos-openai-linear-cursor-raycast-here-is-the-4-beat-43c)
- The Screen Studio look (dark gradient, rounded corners, soft shadow, auto-zoom on clicks) has become shorthand for "polished SaaS demo". Free one-click tools now reproduce it. — [wildandfreetools](https://wildandfreetools.com/blog/screen-studio-alternative-free-polished-demos/); [Screen Studio](https://screen.studio/)
- A review argues auto-zoom can hurt product demos by hiding the full UI. — [joshuasanderford.com](https://joshuasanderford.com/screen-studio)
- Agent skills now generate launch videos from code (Remotion or HyperFrames, cut to music from real screens), which commoditises the template further. — [launch-video-craft](https://github.com/jammaru/launch-video-craft); [product-launch-video](https://github.com/IstiqlalBhat/product-launch-video); [launchvideo.dev](https://www.launchvideo.dev/)

### Inferences
- Clichés to avoid:
  - auto-zoom on every click
  - an untreated dark gradient behind a floating window
  - a stock "inspirational" bed
  - a problem montage of cluttered terminals
  - a closing card of "tagline + URL + logo" with nothing earned before it
  - benchmark charts as the hook (Cursor 2.0 does this, but it works only with a brand behind it)
- What standout examples share:
  - one idea carried through, such as an unbroken pan, a metaphor (branches as light), a sound motif tied to the key action, or radical honesty
  - restraint in narration, with none or a single founder voice
  - a backdrop with character, such as Cursor's painted landscapes, instead of a template gradient
- Agent products face a particular problem: agents are slow and their output is text-heavy. The films people praise compress time (one continuous task with captions in Cursor 2.0, 2–3× speed in the vendor spec) rather than showing an unedited wait. Founders or engineers on camera stand in for "trust" (Cursor 3, Warp, Antigravity).

### Gaps
- I found no independent design-critic essay arguing "all AI launch videos look the same". The cliché claim rests on the template's ubiquity and vendor framing, not a cited critique.
- Cursor treatment (an enlarged smoothed cursor against a hidden one) was not covered by any source.
